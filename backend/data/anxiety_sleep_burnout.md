# Anxiety, sleep and burnout

Anxiety shows up in the body: racing heart, tight chest, shallow breathing, stomach knots, restlessness. These are signs of your nervous system's alarm response, not signs that something is wrong with you.

Slow breathing with a longer exhale calms that alarm. Try breathing in for 4 counts and out for 6 counts, for about two minutes. The Breathe tool on this site guides you through it.

Grounding helps when thoughts are spiralling: name 5 things you can see, 4 you can feel, 3 you can hear, 2 you can smell and 1 you can taste. It pulls attention back to the present.

For sleep: keep a consistent wake-up time even on weekends, stop screens 30-60 minutes before bed, avoid caffeine after mid-afternoon, and don't lie in bed awake for more than ~20 minutes. Get up, do something calm in dim light, then try again.

Burnout feels like exhaustion that rest doesn't fix, cynicism about your work, and a sense that nothing you do matters. It builds when demand stays high and recovery stays low for too long.

Recovery means protecting non-negotiable rest, saying no to one extra commitment, and reconnecting with something you do purely for enjoyment. If burnout persists for weeks, talk to a counsellor or your faculty advisor about your load.
