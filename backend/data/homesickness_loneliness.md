# Homesickness, loneliness and fitting in

Missing home is normal, especially in your first year or after a vacation. It often comes in waves: around festivals, when you're unwell, or when the hostel is quiet.

Keep a light routine of contact with family, like a fixed call a few times a week, rather than being on the phone all day. Too much contact can make settling in harder; too little can make you feel cut off.

Loneliness on a busy campus can feel confusing. Many students feel this way but assume everyone else has found their group already. That assumption is usually wrong.

Low-pressure ways to connect: join one club or society that matches a genuine interest, eat meals in the mess at regular times, study in common areas instead of your room, or say yes to one plan a week even when you'd rather stay in.

Friendships form through repeated, small contact over time. It's okay if it takes a semester.

If loneliness turns into feeling empty most days, losing interest in things you used to enjoy, or withdrawing completely, that can be a sign of depression, and talking to a counsellor can really help.
